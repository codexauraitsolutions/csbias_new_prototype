One Nation, One Election: what the committee recommends.

Dr. R. Krishna Murthy · Polity faculty

On this page
  • Why in news
  • Background
  • Key recommendations
  • Arguments in favour
  • Concerns
  • Practice question
Why in news? The Union Cabinet accepted the High-Level Committee's recommendations on simultaneous elections, and constitutional amendment bills have been introduced in Parliament.

Background

Simultaneous elections to the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies were the norm until 1967. The cycle broke due to premature dissolution of several assemblies and of the Lok Sabha itself. Since then, the country has seen elections in some part of it almost every year.

Key recommendations

  • Simultaneous elections for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies as the first step.
  • Municipal and panchayat elections within 100 days of general elections.
  • A single electoral roll and voter ID for all three tiers.
  • Fresh elections only for the unexpired term after a hung house.
Frequent elections impose a huge burden on the exchequer and disrupt governance.

Arguments in favour

Reduced expenditure, policy continuity, less frequent deployment of security forces and teachers, and potentially higher voter turnout.

Concerns

Impact on federalism, national issues overshadowing local ones, the logistics of EVMs and VVPATs, and the complexity of curtailing assembly terms.

Practice question

“Simultaneous elections will strengthen governance but weaken federalism.” Critically examine. (15 marks, 250 words)
Key takeaways
  • Two-step rollout
  • 100-day window for local polls
  • Single electoral roll
  • Needs constitutional amendment
